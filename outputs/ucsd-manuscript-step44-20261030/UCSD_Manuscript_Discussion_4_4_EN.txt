The computational contribution of this study is an auditable evaluation of low-dimensional feature-level fusion using public clinical and MRI data. Eight explicitly defined descriptors from FLAIR and postcontrast T1 images were concatenated with five clinical inputs and fitted with a fixed L2-regularized logistic model. This design makes the imaging contribution traceable to specified intensity and texture calculations and anchors the comparison to the same clinical input set. Evaluating all models on matched examinations with shared patient-grouped folds reduces the influence of case composition and partition differences on the incremental comparison. Training-fold preprocessing and retained OOF probabilities also make the relationship between inputs, fitted models and evaluation outputs inspectable. The public repository provides notebooks, derived feature tables, fold assignments and analysis locks, allowing model evaluation to be rerun from the saved features. Re-extracting features additionally requires the image files and their documented acquisition or restoration routes. This emphasis on processing details, comparator models, partitioning and code availability is consistent with methodological priorities described in METRICS (11). These contributions position the work as an application and evaluation study in medical image computing. They facilitate scrutiny of the reported increment, while the reference-standard and external-validation limitations still constrain its interpretation.
