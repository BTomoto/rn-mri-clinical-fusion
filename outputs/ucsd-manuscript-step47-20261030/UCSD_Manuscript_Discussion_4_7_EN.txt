The single-scan analysis leaves Fusion's incremental discrimination uncertain under the selected sampling scheme. Its conditional paired AUC-difference interval ranged from -0.015 to 0.252, accommodating a small disadvantage as well as a larger gain. Selection retained the same 135 patients but reduced treatment-related-change examinations from 41 to 22 and changed the label distribution, training samples and fold assignments. These concurrent changes prevent attributing the difference from the primary analysis solely to repeated scans. The smallest available numeric session also did not establish the first clinical visit. At the fixed threshold, Fusion correctly recognized only 2/22 treatment-related-change examinations, despite identifying most tumor examinations. This class-specific limitation remains relevant even when ranking performance is summarized by AUC. Equalizing contributions to one examination per patient does not create a cohort independent of the primary analysis or verify the source diagnoses. Guidance for external validation emphasizes a dataset representative of the target population and sample-size planning for precise performance estimates (15,16). Accordingly, the selected analysis is best interpreted as an internal sensitivity check. Confirmation of the increment requires independent data with adequate representation of both outcomes and evaluation of discrimination, calibration and class-specific performance.
