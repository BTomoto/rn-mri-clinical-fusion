Background
Distinguishing residual or recurrent tumor from treatment-related change after high-grade glioma treatment remains difficult. We evaluated whether fixed magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) descriptors provide incremental discrimination beyond available clinical inputs in public data.

Methods
This secondary analysis of the public UCSD-PTGBM collection included 182 examinations from 135 patients. Four fixed L2-penalized logistic models used age and sex, five clinical inputs, eight MRI descriptors, or their fusion. MRI features summarized fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) and postcontrast T1 intensity and texture within supplied annotation regions. Five-fold patient-grouped validation generated out-of-fold predictions. The primary comparison was the Fusion-minus-expanded-clinical area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) difference. 2000 patient-cluster bootstrap attempts provided intervals conditional on fixed predictions; boundary, repeated-split and single-scan analyses assessed sensitivity.

Results
Fusion achieved an AUC of 0.782 versus 0.672 for the expanded clinical model and 0.762 for MRI alone. The paired increment was 0.110 (95% percentile interval, 0.021 to 0.214) and remained positive across 20 repeated partitions. In the single-scan analysis, Fusion AUC was 0.730 and its increment was uncertain (0.120; interval, -0.015 to 0.252). At the fixed 0.5 threshold, primary Fusion recognized 130/141 tumor examinations but only 12/41 treatment-change examinations. Its descriptive calibration slope was 0.667; probability-error difference intervals included zero.

Conclusions
Clinical-MRI fusion showed an internal incremental signal, with uncertain single-scan benefit and limited treatment-change recognition. Independent evaluation is needed before inferring clinical usefulness. The model has not undergone external validation or clinical utility evaluation.

Keywords: high-grade glioma; treatment-related change; magnetic resonance imaging; feature fusion; logistic regression; internal validation
