Boundary perturbation and repeated partitioning probe different sources of sensitivity. For Fusion, the eroded-minus-original AUC difference was 0.0062, with a conditional paired interval spanning zero. Preserving the original normalization and binning references while eroding the ROI suggests limited sensitivity to that specific boundary change under the fixed workflow. The result does not demonstrate equivalence or segmentation accuracy. Martin et al. examined scan-rescan repeatability of diffusion and perfusion radiomics (13), a measurement property that our erosion analysis does not test. Repeated patient-grouped validation instead examined sensitivity to the allocation of patients across training folds. The Fusion-minus-M1 AUC difference remained positive in all 20 partitions, supporting directional consistency within the available cohort. Because the same patients and labels were reused, the repetitions were neither independent replications nor external validation. Patient grouping addresses the dependence risk illustrated by Rosenblatt et al. using duplicated scans in connectome prediction (14), although that experiment does not quantify bias in our diagnostic task. Together, the analyses strengthen the internal sensitivity assessment while leaving uncertainty about source annotations, reference diagnoses and performance at other institutions unresolved. The original ROI and prespecified primary partition remain the basis of the main comparison.
