Availability-based sampling restricts the population represented by this analysis. MRI inputs were available for 182 of 238 eligible clinical examinations, leaving 56 examinations outside the paired model comparison. The included 135 patients and 41 treatment-related-change examinations provide limited coverage of the diagnostic spectrum; performance may differ in unavailable cases or other clinical settings. Repeated examinations increased observations without creating independent patients. Because fitting and pooled performance gave each examination equal weight, patients with more examinations contributed more than patients with a single scan, even though grouping prevented patient overlap between folds. Fusion also estimated 13 input coefficients from a modest cohort. Fixed regularization and the absence of tuning reduced analytical flexibility but did not establish that estimation was sufficiently stable. The initial-event interval was missing for 36 examinations and referred to heterogeneous starting events. Training-fold median imputation and a missingness indicator could not recover that history or establish an ignorable missingness mechanism. Finally, the patient-cluster bootstrap resampled fixed out-of-fold predictions without refitting. Its intervals therefore describe patient-sampling uncertainty conditional on those predictions, excluding variation from repeating model development. Repeated grouped splits assessed another aspect of instability within the same data; neither procedure substitutes for independent evaluation.
