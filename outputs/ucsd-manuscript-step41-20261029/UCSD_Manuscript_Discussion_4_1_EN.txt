In this secondary analysis of public data, adding eight fixed MRI descriptors to five clinical inputs improved internal discrimination between residual or recurrent tumor and treatment-related change after treatment for high-grade glioma. Fusion achieved an OOF AUC of 0.782, compared with 0.672 for M1, with a paired difference of 0.110 (95% patient-bootstrap percentile interval, 0.021 to 0.214). This comparison used the same examinations and patient-grouped folds, limiting differences attributable to case selection or partition assignment. The AUC increment remained positive across all 20 repeated partitions, although these repetitions reused the same cohort and provided descriptive evidence of split stability. Incremental discrimination was less certain when one available examination per patient was retained: the AUC-difference interval extended from -0.015 to 0.252. These intervals were conditional on fixed OOF predictions and did not capture full retraining variability. Moreover, Fusion recognized only 12/41 treatment-related-change examinations in the primary analysis and 2/22 in the single-scan analysis at the fixed threshold. Its primary calibration slope of 0.667, together with intervals crossing zero for the Brier-score and logarithmic-loss differences, also limited claims about probability accuracy. The findings therefore support an incremental MRI signal within this dataset, while leaving its clinical usefulness and performance in independent cohorts unresolved.
