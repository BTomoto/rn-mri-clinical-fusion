We performed a retrospective secondary analysis to evaluate whether quantitative magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) features added discriminatory information to clinical variables for distinguishing residual or recurrent tumor from treatment-related change on post-treatment glioma examinations. The target was the diagnostic status assigned to each MRI examination. Clinical, MRI, and combined models were evaluated in a common cohort using cross-validation with patients kept separate between training and validation folds. The examination was the unit of prediction, and all examinations used for modeling from a given patient were assigned to the same fold. The analysis used publicly available, de-identified records and images, without participant recruitment, patient contact, additional imaging, or specimen collection. The dataset authors reported institutional review board approval and a waiver of informed consent for the original data collection (1). Reporting was organized with reference to TRIPOD+AI guidance for prediction models and the Checklist for Artificial Intelligence in Medical Imaging (CLAIM) (2, 3).

The source collection was the University of California San Diego Post-Treatment Glioblastoma (UCSD-PTGBM) dataset, distributed through The Cancer Imaging Archive under a Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license (1). We used the public version 3 clinical workbook and the accompanying list of examinations with treatment-related changes. Imaging inputs were obtained from the publicly accessible data-nih/tcia release mirror on GitHub and comprised derived fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) volumes, postcontrast T1-weighted volumes, and existing BraTS annotation masks in Neuroimaging Informatics Technology Initiative format. Source clinical identifiers, BraTS identifiers, mirror filenames, and the correspondence between them were retained in the linkage records. The mirror files were treated as a specific derived input release. Their observed intensity representation and header metadata determined the processing rules used in this analysis. Download addresses, file sizes, and SHA256 checksums were retained to identify the exact files used and support restoration of the analysis inputs.

The published source collection contained 243 examinations from 178 patients (1). An identifier audit identified discordant relationships between the clinical and BraTS patient identifiers in five examinations from two source patients. These examinations were quarantined before clinical-model revalidation, leaving 238 examinations from 176 patients in the clinical cohort. Of these, 182 examinations from 135 patients had corresponding FLAIR images, postcontrast T1-weighted images, and annotation masks in the mirror and yielded the complete eight-feature MRI matrix. The remaining 56 clinical examinations were retained in the coverage records but did not enter the imaging-model comparison because the required mirror inputs were unavailable. The final imaging cohort comprised 141 examinations labeled as residual or recurrent tumor and 41 labeled as treatment-related change. Cohort assembly is summarized in Figure 1, and clinical characteristics by imaging coverage are provided in Supplementary Table S1. All four primary models were evaluated on this same imaging cohort.
