Published evidence does not show a uniform benefit from adding imaging information. Leone et al. evaluated 105 patients with IDH-wildtype glioblastoma, including 20 with pseudoprogression, using five repetitions of five-fold cross-validation (9). A clinical-MGMT reference model achieved an AUC of 0.69; adding median diffusion and perfusion measures produced 0.71 without a statistically significant improvement. Models incorporating radiomic features yielded AUCs of 0.52 and 0.54. Their baseline included MGMT status and extent of resection, which were absent from our M1, and outcomes largely relied on subsequent imaging and clinical evolution. Consequently, the differing results do not isolate an effect of model complexity or establish why fusion did or did not help. A separate study by de Godoy et al. applied a previously developed diffusion-perfusion model to 56 new patients and reported an AUC of 0.757 (10). At their 0.5 probability threshold, it correctly identified 18/19 pseudoprogression cases and 21/37 cases in the progression-plus-mixed-tumor group. However, mixed tumors were grouped with pseudoprogression during development and with progression during validation, changing the classification task. This was evaluation in a new cohort, but it did not test our features or model. These studies show why discrimination, class-specific recognition and endpoint alignment require separate scrutiny. For our analysis, the uncertain single-scan increment and limited treatment-change recognition remain material findings, despite the favorable primary AUC difference. Further evaluation should preserve the intended reference categories and assess a locked model in an independent cohort.
