Calibration was assessed descriptively using the pooled OOF tumor probabilities. For each model, we requested five probability quantile groups, dropping duplicate boundaries and retaining the actual nonempty groups. Each group was summarized by its examination count, mean predicted probability, observed tumor proportion and tumor count. Calibration-in-the-large was estimated as an intercept with the coefficient of the predicted log odds fixed at one (Equation 9). A separate unpenalized logistic diagnostic regression estimated a free intercept and calibration slope jointly (Equation 10). Here, p denotes the saved OOF probability and q the fitted diagnostic probability. For logit calculations only, p was clipped to machine epsilon and one minus machine epsilon. The offset intercept was distinguished from the joint intercept accompanying the slope. Failed or separated fits were retained as missing estimates with their recorded errors. These diagnostics used the same OOF predictions being evaluated and did not constitute independent validation. We did not use them to recalibrate predictions, alter the fixed classification threshold or replace any evaluated OOF probabilities.
