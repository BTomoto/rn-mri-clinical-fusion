We fitted four binary logistic regression models with an intercept. M0 included age and sex; M1 included the five clinical inputs defined above and served as the principal clinical comparator. The MRI model used the eight fixed imaging features, and Fusion concatenated the five clinical inputs and eight MRI features into one 13-variable predictor vector. This was feature-level fusion within a single model. Equation 7 gives the tumor probability for the processed feature vector z, where m denotes the input count and the beta terms denote the intercept and coefficients. The four models contained 2, 5, 8 and 13 nonintercept terms, respectively. All used L2 regularization with inverse regularization strength C set to 1, the lbfgs optimizer, a maximum of 2000 iterations and convergence tolerance 0.0001. No class weighting was applied. A common regression framework provided a consistent comparison of the fixed input sets. Coefficients were used for prediction and were not interpreted as causal effects.

All four models were evaluated in the same 182-examination cohort using the same five patient-grouped folds. We used StratifiedGroupKFold with shuffling enabled and random seed 20261029, grouping examinations by the audited patient-group identifier. All examinations from a given patient were kept in one fold, and separation was checked using both the source clinical and BraTS patient identifiers. The case-to-fold manifest was fixed before model fitting and was not reselected after viewing performance. Age, the transformed interval and MRI features were standardized within training folds wherever included; interval imputation followed Section 2.3. Binary inputs were not scaled. Fitted preprocessing and regression parameters were applied to the held-out fold without using its outcomes to train or select the model. Each examination received one out-of-fold (OOF) tumor probability per model. There was no hyperparameter search, feature selection, dimensionality reduction, oversampling or interaction-term expansion. Validation was internal to the public collection.
