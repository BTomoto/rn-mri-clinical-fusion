Image processing limits the portability and biological interpretation of the observed signal. The supplied structural volumes were stored as unsigned 8-bit integers, so subsequent scaling could not recover distinctions absent from those derived values. Unknown spatial units also prevented physical-volume interpretation and defining texture neighborhoods in verified millimeters. Matching image and mask grids supported computation at corresponding voxel indices, but did not independently establish anatomical registration. Our positive-label union pooled the supplied annotation compartments, potentially combining different tissue and treatment effects within a single summary. No compartment-specific diagnostic claim follows from its predictive performance. We replaced percentile clipping with unclipped affine scaling after technical diagnostics revealed additional tail collapse. This documented engineering revision avoided further clipping, but was neither preregistered nor independently shown to be optimal. Evidence that normalization choices influence MRI feature comparability across acquisition settings reinforces the need for evaluation under other processing conditions (12). It does not validate our transform, and ROI-range fixed-bin-number texture features need not change under every positive affine transformation. Finally, the eight descriptors used only FLAIR and postcontrast T1 images. Quantitative ADC and perfusion information were not evaluated. The findings therefore concern this particular derived-image representation; confirmation with preserved acquisition metadata, verified spatial scales and standardized processing remains necessary.
