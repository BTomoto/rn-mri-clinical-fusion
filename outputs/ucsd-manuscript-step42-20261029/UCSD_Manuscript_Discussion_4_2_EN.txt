The observed increment has parallels in studies combining quantitative MRI with non-imaging information, although their predictor sets differ substantially from ours. In a single-center cohort of 222 patients with grade 3 or 4 glioma, Zhou et al. integrated a 17-feature radiomics score with relative cerebral blood volume, apparent diffusion coefficient, neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio, MGMT promoter methylation and temozolomide treatment (6). Their stratified 7:3 internal split yielded validation AUCs of 0.811 for the integrated model and 0.744 for the model excluding the radiomics score. That comparison therefore assessed radiomics beyond a baseline already containing advanced imaging and molecular information. McKenney et al. studied 74 patients with IDH-wildtype glioblastoma who underwent repeat surgery, using contrast-enhanced T1 and dynamic contrast-enhanced perfusion radiomics with MGMT status (7). Their repeated nested cross-validation showed higher accuracy for the combined classifier than for radiomics alone, with lesion labels determined by histopathology. These reports motivate evaluating complementary information but do not establish that our limited clinical inputs or eight MRI descriptors capture the same signal. Our outcome also encompasses broader treatment-related change, limiting alignment with their pseudoprogression endpoints. Related work by Reecher et al. used UCSD-PTGBM itself (8); its shared parent dataset precludes treating it as independent confirmation. Our study provides a paired assessment of fixed, low-dimensional clinical-MRI fusion on matched examinations. Differences in patient selection, reference standards, input availability and validation designs prevent direct ranking of our model against these reports by AUC.
