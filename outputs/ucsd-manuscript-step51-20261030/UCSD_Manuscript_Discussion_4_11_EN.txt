Fusion's AUC of 0.782 indicates discrimination within this dataset, but does not establish reliable individual probabilities or a decision rule. Metrics Reloaded distinguishes classification at a threshold, discrimination across thresholds and calibration as complementary dimensions of evaluation (17). Here, the descriptive calibration slope of 0.667 raises concern about the spread of predicted probabilities, while the paired Brier-score and logarithmic-loss difference intervals included zero. At the unchanged threshold of 0.5, Fusion identified 130/141 tumor examinations but only 12/41 treatment-related-change examinations. For a task intended to distinguish treatment effects from tumor, the 29 treatment-change examinations classified as tumor remain a relevant limitation. These counts do not quantify the consequences of using the model, because no treatment decisions or downstream outcomes were evaluated. Nor does the small accuracy advantage over assigning all examinations to tumor establish clinical value. A clinically defensible threshold would require a defined action and consideration of the consequences of each type of error. Guidance recommends assessing net benefit across relevant, prespecified thresholds when predictions are intended to direct care (15). We did not perform that evaluation, recalibrate on independent data or compare the model with clinician decisions. The present evidence supports further evaluation of an incremental imaging signal, without establishing that model-guided care improves outcomes.
