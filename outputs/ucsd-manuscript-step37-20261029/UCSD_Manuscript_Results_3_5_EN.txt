Fusion had an offset calibration intercept of -0.121 and a calibration slope of 0.667 in the pooled OOF predictions (Figure 2C; Supplementary Table S3). These were descriptive calibration diagnostics and were not applied to modify the evaluated probabilities. At the fixed tumor-probability threshold of 0.5, Fusion correctly classified 130 of 141 tumor examinations (92.2%) and 12 of 41 treatment-related-change examinations (29.3%). Eleven tumor examinations were classified as treatment-related change, whereas 29 treatment-related-change examinations were classified as tumor. Overall accuracy was 142/182 (78.0%), compared with 141/182 (77.5%) for a reference rule assigning every examination to tumor. Balanced accuracy was 0.607. M1 correctly recognized two treatment-related-change examinations, while MRI and Fusion each recognized 12. The same MRI and Fusion models correctly classified 128 and 130 tumor examinations, respectively. Thus, the higher discrimination estimates coexisted with limited treatment-related-change recognition at the unchanged threshold, and overall accuracy remained close to the always-tumor reference.
