The examination-level outcome was coded as 1 for residual or recurrent tumor and 0 for treatment-related change. We matched the 51 entries in the public negative-case workbook to the clinical records by examination identifier and assigned them to class 0. Class 1 comprised the remaining 192 examinations, using the published partition of the full 243-examination collection (1). These class assignments were retained after identifier quarantine and restriction to examinations with available MRI inputs. The source reference standard combined pathological evidence with clinical and imaging assessment; the dataset authors reported pathological confirmation for 45 of the 192 tumor examinations in the full collection (1). Treatment-related change encompassed pseudoprogression, radiation necrosis and nonspecific post-treatment changes. Subtype counts in the frozen workbook differed from the published summary, although the overall binary counts agreed. We therefore retained the original subtype entries for audit and modeled the broad binary outcome. We carried forward the source classifications without independent diagnostic adjudication. This outcome represented the status assigned to the examination and did not define subsequent recurrence or a future radiation-necrosis event.
