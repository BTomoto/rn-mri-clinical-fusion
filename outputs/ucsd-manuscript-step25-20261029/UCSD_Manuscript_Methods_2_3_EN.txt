We used four clinical variables from the public clinical workbook: age, sex at birth, the interval from the initial clinical event to the examination, and other treatment before the scan (1). Age was entered as a continuous variable in years at the time of scanning. Sex was encoded as 1 for male and 0 for female. The other-treatment field was encoded as 1 for Yes and 0 for No, retaining its broad source definition without assigning a specific treatment modality or drug. An additional binary indicator identified a missing initial-event interval, with 1 denoting missing and 0 denoting observed. These encodings provided two inputs for the age-and-sex model and five clinical inputs for both the expanded clinical model and the fusion model. The same variable definitions were used throughout the comparisons. Follow-up, survival outcomes and diagnostic category fields were excluded from the clinical inputs. Clinical input definitions are summarized in Supplementary Table S2.

The source time field expressed the initial-event date as a signed day offset relative to MRI acquisition. We accepted only strictly negative numeric offsets, reversed their signs to obtain positive elapsed days, and applied the natural logarithm of one plus the interval. The reference event could be initial surgery, another initial treatment, or diagnosis in an untreated patient, so the resulting variable did not represent a uniform interval since radiotherapy or surgery. Nonnumeric entries, blank values and nonnegative offsets were treated as missing; a zero offset did not establish that the event preceded the examination. In the 182-examination imaging cohort, 36 intervals were missing under this rule. Within each training fold, missing transformed intervals were replaced with the median observed transformed interval, followed by standardization. Age was also standardized within the training fold. Learned medians and scaling parameters were applied unchanged to the corresponding validation fold. Binary inputs were passed through without scaling. Age, sex and the other-treatment indicator were complete in the imaging cohort.
