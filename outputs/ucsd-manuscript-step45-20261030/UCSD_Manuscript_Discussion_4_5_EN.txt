The eight MRI inputs summarize different statistical aspects of the supplied annotation region. Mean intensity describes its average percentile-scaled signal, and standard deviation describes the spread of those values. Histogram entropy summarizes uncertainty in the discretized gray-level distribution without retaining voxel positions. GLCM contrast weights squared gray-level differences between neighboring voxels and therefore incorporates local spatial variation (4). These descriptors capture aspects of regional image appearance, with their interpretation depending on the supplied images and extraction rules. The union of all positive annotation labels does not isolate a histologically verified viable-tumor compartment. Duenweg et al. showed that normalization choices affected radiomic comparability across acquisition settings, including in brain imaging (12). Their findings do not establish an optimal transformation for our derived images. Here, percentile scaling and ROI-based discretization define the numerical context of the features; unknown spatial units further limit interpretation of texture at a physical distance. The fitted coefficients describe conditional associations within a penalized model, with their interpretation depending on feature scaling and the other included inputs. They do not establish independent risk factors or causal effects. Accordingly, the observed incremental discrimination supports a collective predictive signal within this dataset, without identifying a specific cellular mechanism.
